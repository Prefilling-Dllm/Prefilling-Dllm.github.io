01 · Motivation

Every denoising step re-reads a prefix it barely looks at.

A diffusion language model runs full attention over the prompt and the response at every denoising step, so the cost grows with the square of the context and repeats for each step.

Two measurements on Dream-7B say most of that work is wasted. Attention from the response to the prefix decays quickly with distance, more so in later steps, with a few periodic spikes at segment boundaries. And once the context is extrapolated beyond 4K, accuracy depends on where the evidence sits: only documents near the response are found.

02 · Method

Prefill the prefix once. Decode against the few chunks that matter.

Prefilling-dLLM brings prefill-decode disaggregation to diffusion language models without any training: the prefix KV cache is computed once per chunk, ranked, pruned, and reused unchanged by every denoising step.

STAGE IPrefillSplit the prefix into N chunks of C tokens, each opened by a BOS token, and run each with the query and a short draft answer in its own bidirectional forward pass.
STAGE IISparse attentionKeep the K best-scoring chunks, and inside each the B tokens that exchange the most attention with the query.
STAGE IIIDecodeUnmask the response over T steps. Only the response tokens are processed; the KV of the selected chunks and of the query is reused as cached.
O((Lp + Lq + Ld)² · T) → O(N(C + Lq + |m|)² + (Ld² + K·C) · T) The first term is chunk scoring and cache construction with the query and the draft answer m; the second is denoising over the selected prefix cache. The per-step cost no longer depends on the prefix length Lp.
/ 01

Predictive score

A chunk is ranked by how well it predicts the query and a short draft answer: the self-information of the query given the chunk, plus the likelihood of pseudo-labels from one denoising pass. Both are length-normalized and weighted equally.

s(c) = sI(c) + sP(c)

/ 02

Inter-chunk sparsity

Only the top-K chunks take part in decoding, with K much smaller than N. The main Dream-7B setting keeps 4 chunks of 1,024 tokens whatever the input length.

/ 03

Intra-chunk sparsity

Inside a selected chunk, tokens are ranked by the attention they exchange with the query in both directions, and the top B = 512 stay in the cache.

04 · Efficiency

The longer the context, the larger the gain.

Decoding always runs over the same compressed budget of about 4K tokens, so its time stays flat while every baseline keeps paying for the full context at each step.

05 · Analysis

Chunk BOS tokens anchor attention instead of sinking it.

Five findings from the paper explain why selecting chunks removes the positional bias rather than moving it.

  1. 1Sharding the selected KV speeds up decoding.After the top-K chunks are chosen, their cached KV is split into S compute shards that are attended in parallel and merged by a log-sum-exp reduction: 5.8–10.2× lower decode latency.
  2. 2Lost-in-the-middle is eliminated.Chunks are chosen by predictive score rather than by proximity, and the periodic attention spikes at chunk BOS tokens become the signal for position-invariant retrieval.
  3. 3Chunk BOS tokens act as periodic anchors.They spread attention mass across the context instead of collapsing it onto one token.
  4. 4The anchors stay small.All nine chunk BOS tokens together absorb 2.72% of the attention; a single BOS in an autoregressive model takes 20–60%.
  5. 5Chunk size trades quality for compute.Under a fixed 4,096-token budget, 256-token chunks hold 92.3% exact match at 128K, while a single 4,096-token chunk degrades sharply beyond 32K.

06 · What came next

v1 selects chunks around a frozen model. v2 teaches the model to route.

Prefilling-dLLM fixes its top-K chunks after prefill. The paper lists this among its limitations, and finds static selection stronger for retaining exact evidence than for aggregation-heavy summarization. The follow-up, Prefilling-dLLM v2, moves the selection inside the model.
